What Inspires Me

“I am interested in the quiet moment when a difficult problem begins to make sense.”

What inspires me most is the process of turning confusion into understanding. A program rarely works perfectly on the first attempt, and that is part of what makes software engineering meaningful to me. Each error is a clue, each revision is a small improvement, and each working solution is proof that patience can turn an idea into something useful.

I am also motivated by the thought that technology is built through shared effort. Listening to different ideas, explaining my own thinking and learning from teammates remind me that good software is not only about writing code; it is also about curiosity, responsibility and collaboration.

My personal reminder is simple: stay curious, test your assumptions, and let consistent practice do its work.

AASTU Parking Management System — Group Project

Screenshot: the team project running in the terminal.

This team project was developed as part of our Fundamentals of Programming work. It is a console-based parking management application written in C++. The repository is organised into separate admin and customer source/header files, shared utility files, a main program and an SQL schema.

The running screenshot shows the registration flow, first-user administrator registration, login and the choice of standard or VIP membership.

Working on this project helped me understand how a larger program can be divided into modules and how team members can contribute to a shared codebase.
